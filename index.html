WIKISOURCE-SOURCED BASELINE

Analects · Book VI, section 3

The Chinese source text and baseline reference come from Wikisource. The English translation and interpretation below are presented as the project's translation of that source passage.

Standardized / simplified Chinese · full passage
子华使于齐，冉子为其母请粟。子曰：“与之釜。”请益，曰：“与之庾。”冉子与之粟五秉。子曰：“赤之适齐也，乘肥马，衣轻裘；吾闻之也：君子周急不继富。”原思为之宰，与之粟九百，辞。子曰：“毋！以与尔邻里乡党乎！”
Pinyin · full passage
Zǐhuá shǐ yú Qí, Rǎn zǐ wéi qí mǔ qǐng sù. Zǐ yuē: “Yǔ zhī fǔ.” Qǐng yì, yuē: “Yǔ zhī yǔ.” Rǎn zǐ yǔ zhī sù wǔ bǐng. Zǐ yuē: “Chì zhī shì Qí yě, chéng féi mǎ, yì qīng qiú; wú wén zhī yě: jūnzǐ zhōu jí bù jì fù.” Yuán Sī wéi zhī zǎi, yǔ zhī sù jiǔ bǎi, cí. Zǐ yuē: “Wú! Yǐ yǔ ěr lín lǐ xiāng dǎng hū!”
English translation · full passage
Zihua was sent on a mission to Qi, and Ran Zi requested grain for his mother. Confucius said, “Give her one fu.” Ran Zi asked for more, and Confucius said, “Give her one yu.” Ran Zi then gave her five bing of grain. Confucius said, “When Chi went to Qi, he rode a fine horse and wore a light fur robe. I have heard that a gentleman helps those in urgent need rather than adding to the wealth of those who are already well supplied.” Yuan Si served as steward and was given nine hundred measures of grain, but he declined them. Confucius said, “Do not refuse them; give them to your neighbors and people in your local community.”
Meaning / cultural context · full passage
The complete passage contrasts appropriate relief with excess. Confucius accepts giving a limited amount to a person in need, but criticizes giving substantial additional resources to someone who is already well supplied. The final exchange extends the idea: resources that are legitimately received can be shared with neighbors and the surrounding community. The ancient measures—釜 (fu), 庾 (yu), and 秉 (bing)—are preserved rather than treated as modern units.

BILINGUAL OPEN-ENDED

CC-Eval · value alignment

Parallel Chinese and English prompts designed to examine whether responses shift when the same value-oriented question is posed in different languages.

  • Compare response framing across languages
  • Useful for saved model-output analysis
  • Compare interpretations and reference context
CHINESE-CONTEXT TASKS

CC-Eval · cultural context

Separate task families for classical Chinese, folk culture, aesthetics/philosophy, internet slang, and pragmatic intent.

  • Explore meaning beyond literal translation
  • Test indirect intent and cultural references
  • Compare task types without treating them as one score

Important: these datasets should not be collapsed into one accuracy number. Their question formats, reference material, labels, and evaluation purposes differ. The explorer keeps those structures visible.
